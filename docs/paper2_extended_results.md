## S9.6 Band and occipital controls

Band and occipital controls (F37/F47, G1–G3; main-text Fig. 7B): the C3/C4 beta rule exceeded the mu rule in the three datasets with occipital channels (+0.018 acute, +0.048 lower-limb, +0.048 paretic-hand); the occipital-alpha rule alone was significantly above 0.5 (acute 0.641, lower-limb 0.604, paretic-hand 0.637), differing from the central beta rule by +0.007 (CI −0.029 to +0.045) under the acute cue reference and by +0.082 and +0.129 (surviving correction) in the lower-limb and paretic-hand data. The conditional predictive increment LDA(occipital-alpha mean) → LDA(occipital-alpha mean + C3/C4 beta) was +0.043 (acute cue, not surviving correction), +0.073 (acute break), +0.112 (lower-limb) and +0.149 (paretic-hand; the last three surviving correction)—this increment controls only one summary of occipital alpha and does not identify the origin of the central-beta information. Lateralisation by imagined hand (G1, G3; main-text Fig. 7A): the present C3/C4 power measures detected no contralateral advantage surviving correction (contralateral − ipsilateral: acute −0.004 [−0.018, +0.011], break reference +0.019; chronic +0.008 [+0.002, +0.016], Holm p = 0.11); left-vs-right AUC of the fixed score −(C3 − C4) was 0.513 (acute; deviation from 0.5 CI −0.009 to +0.035) and 0.527 (chronic, Holm p = 0.054), in the same direction as the CSP left/right re-analysis in S2 and the 48.3–53.1% accuracy of the CSP/FBCSP pipelines of Cruz et al. (Cruz et al., 2025) (their EEGNet reached 69.4–71.9%; AUC and accuracy are not the same quantity); LDA on the same features gave 0.450 in the acute data, significantly below 0.5 (Holm p = 0.047)—the fixed score is unaffected, the anomaly is compatible with anticorrelation from fold-wise class-prior shifts in LDA, and scores were not flipped post hoc. EOG-regression control for the Riemannian classifier (G4/G5): under the cue reference, raw 0.790 → with one extra 0.5–40 Hz causal filter stage (including repeated notch and padding) 0.943 (+0.152 [0.122, 0.184], 49/1 patients) → with EOG regression after that stage 0.798 (regression relative to the matched filter-only control −0.145 [−0.175, −0.116], 2/48 patients, Holm p < 0.001; net difference relative to raw +0.008); the rule went 0.649 → 0.671 → 0.655 (regression relative to filter-only −0.016). Under the break reference the three values were 0.789 → 0.788 → 0.779 (regression relative to filter-only −0.009, Holm p = 0.11), rule 0.623 → 0.623 → 0.644. The Riemannian classifier is thus highly sensitive to the preprocessing chain under the cue reference: one extra filter stage raised it by 0.15 and EOG regression under the same filtering lowered it by 0.15, the two cancelling; the sources of the two steps are separated in the targeted-check section: the first is the start-up transient of the 0.5 Hz high-pass (it disappears with 3-s padding or zero-phase filtering), and the second was repeated with 3-s padding (targeted-check section).


## S9.7 Detectability is a range, not a number

Under the four main settings the fixed rule's mean per-patient AUC was 0.601–0.771 (lowest in severely impaired chronic patients with 11 channels and daily sessions; highest for upper-limb MI versus fixation-period rest), with above-0.7 proportions from 1/14 to 17/24. This is a range of patient means under four analysis settings, not a range of individual AUCs nor a bound on MI detectability after stroke; population severity, the nature and visual content of the reference period, channel count and analysis input all differ and their contributions cannot be separated. A "proportion of detectable stroke patients" reported from one dataset is determined by that dataset's paradigm.


## S9.8 Relation to prior work.* Ang et al. answered how many patients are detectable

*Relation to prior work.* Ang et al. answered how many patients are detectable and tested on separate days (Ang et al., 2011); we make no claim of sample size or priority. Nagarajan et al.'s accuracy is not directly comparable to our AUC (Nagarajan et al., 2024). Cruz et al. analysed paretic-hand MI versus break on the acute dataset with per-subject results (Cruz et al., 2025); we retained all 50 patients and added rule controls, three reference windows and specificity controls, but both studies share the nature of the period labels. In their left-versus-right comparison, CSP and FBCSP reached 48.3–50.7% (left-hand-affected) and 51.5–53.1% (right-hand-affected), whereas EEGNet reached 71.9% and 69.4% [15, Table I]; our near-chance left/right result therefore agrees with their linear spatial-filter pipelines, not with their deep model, and shows only that C3/C4 power features (and the CSP re-analysis in S2) do not separate the hands—not that the recordings contain no decodable left/right information. The benchmark preprint already performed calibration/ranking decomposition and non-motor montage controls (Choi et al., 2026, preprint); our difference lies in within-patient longitudinal data, a fixed score rule, and prefix updating from the reference period only. The Tianjin datasets' original baselines (CSP-SVM 80.50%; CSP 85–86%) refer to MI-versus-idle accuracy and to classification between two MI paradigms and are not comparable to per-patient AUC. The HS Stroke cross-session left/right accuracies of 52–59% (Lu et al., 2026) are a different metric from our period-discrimination AUC.


## S9.9 Pre-specified montages: seven motor-area channels

Pre-specified montages: seven motor-area channels (FC3/FC4/C3/C4/CP3/CP4/Cz) with input-matched LDA and CSP; full-montage absolute log-power LDA and within-window-mean-removed LDA; a **distal nine-channel set** defined anatomically as Fp1/Fp2/F7/F8/P7(T5)/P8(T6)/Oz/O1/O2 (no central, fronto-central or parietal channels) for a distal-mean rule, the central-minus-distal rule, LDA(distal) vs LDA(distal + central) incremental information, and relative-feature LDA; a global (all-channel mean) rule as sensitivity. The chronic dataset has no distal coverage.


## S9.10 The patient was the unit of analysis. Paired comparisons used Wilcoxon signed-rank tests

The patient was the unit of analysis. Paired comparisons used Wilcoxon signed-rank tests (SciPy exact method for n ≤ 25; zero differences dropped; average ranks for ties) with Holm correction within pre-specified families: F1–F3 acute reference windows (F1 main, exploratory choice; F2/F3 sensitivity), F4 chronic within-session, F5 chronic deployment, F6 prefix update, F7 paretic hand, F8 input-matched controls, F9 controls, F10/F11 source sensitivity (two windows), F12/F13 distal reference and incremental information (two windows), F14 early–later association. Mean paired differences with patient-bootstrap 95% intervals (uncorrected) and Hodges–Lehmann location estimates with bootstrap intervals are reported alongside; tests and intervals are not inversions of each other. *Hierarchy of hypotheses.* Primary (confirmatory, Holm-corrected within family): the detector comparisons of main-text Table 2 (rule vs LDA, rule vs CSP, Riemannian vs rule) and the within-minus-fixed comparisons of main-text Table 3. Secondary: the prefix-update gains, the design contrast and the specificity controls. Exploratory: band/occipital, lateralisation, EOG-regression, filter-transient and held-out window-sensitivity analyses. Conclusions in the Discussion are stated at the level of the corresponding tier, and uncorrected intervals are never used to carry a confirmatory claim. Additional families F15–F36 for the Tianjin datasets, numbered in order of appearance in `docs/tianjin_stats.md` (paretic-hand AH/SF within-session per time point and deployment; lower-limb within-session per design and time point, design contrast, deployment), with Hodges–Lehmann estimates reported alongside. A lower-limb sensitivity restricted to runs containing both trial types was identical to the main analysis because all runs were mixed.
